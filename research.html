Featured project · COVID-19 household transmission

How 107.9 million posts became one number

The question was simple. When COVID-19 got into a UK home, how often did it spread to the people living there? This is how the answer came out of social media.

Dhiman et al., npj Digital Medicine 7:194 (2024). First author. Dots show proportions where it helps; they are not individual people.

  1. 1,226,509

    people posted on X about catching COVID-19, or about someone at home catching it.

  2. 77,016

    of them could be placed in the UK.

    6.3% of the first group. The lit dots keep that proportion.

  3. 58,555

    also wrote about the people they live with.

  4. 107.9M

    posts from their timelines, January 2020 to February 2022.

  5. 7,894

    posts labelled by hand to teach the models what to look for.

  6. 0.934

    best AUC of three fine-tuned CT-BERT classifiers.

    Author infected 0.907 · household member infected 0.934 · household mentioned 0.768.

  7. 0.16

    household secondary attack rate (95% CI 0.14–0.18), with 6.49 days between one case and the next.

    About 44% of household infections were never posted about. The model corrects for that.

Featured project · On-device AI for NHS cancer teams

The meeting that never leaves the room

A breast cancer team meets to decide treatment. My system listens, checks NICE guidelines and drafts options on one small computer in the room. The audio never leaves it.

Dhiman et al., arXiv preprint (2026), doi:10.48550/arXiv.2608.22108. Lead and corresponding author. NHS-funded (£41,707, Co-Investigator).

  1. The meeting Illustration

    A breast cancer team meets to agree each patient’s treatment. Everything said is sensitive.

  2. Listen24.4%

    lower word error rate on held-out recordings of real MDT meetings (20.7% on the other set), from Whisper large-v3 with silence-aware preprocessing, Bayesian search with Optuna and noise-adaptive decoding.

    The bar shows the relative cut, not the error rate itself.

  3. Retrieve Illustration

    Each case is matched against NICE guidelines in a local FAISS index, so a suggestion can point to the guidance behind it.

  4. Reason, on one device Illustration27B

    MedGemma 27B, quantised, served with llama.cpp on CUDA and TensorRT inside Docker on one NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin. Audio and transcripts stay on the device, in step with NHS information governance.

  5. The test66

    treatment decisions across six cases, labelled by two oncology experts.

    The grid is not grouped by case.

  6. The result0.318vs0.1362.3×

    On-device MedGemma with retrieval found 0.318 of the MDT-concordant treatments; the cloud chatbot baseline found 0.136. 2.3 times the recall, p = 0.020.

  7. Clinicians stay in charge14

    12 clinicians and 2 MDT coordinators consulted: documentation support and case triage are the near-term uses; workflow integration and governance are the main barriers.
